The Fire Safety Crisis in the Philippines

Fire incidents remain one of the most destructive yet preventable hazards in the country. The Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP) frequently reports multiple severe fire outbreaks during the dry season and holiday peaks, driven by overloaded electrical circuits, unattended open flames, liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) leaks, and substandard residential wiring.

Limitations of Conventional Fire Education

Traditional fire safety advocacy predominantly relies on passive media: physical pamphlets, static posters, and occasional school lecture drills. While informative, these modalities fail to instill actionable decision-making under stress:

  • Zero Cognitive Pressure: Lectures do not simulate time-critical panic, smoke obscurity, or urgency.
  • No Adaptive Feedback: Static materials cannot evaluate whether a participant has truly mastered procedural knowledge (e.g. knowing never to throw water on an electrical or grease fire).
  • Emergency Reporting Hesitancy: Citizens frequently delay reporting fires or fail to relay crucial landmark and hazard information to 911/BFP dispatchers.

The Dispatch Simulator Solution

By positioning the learner in the seat of an emergency communications officer in Santa Cruz, Laguna, The Right Call reverses passive learning into active cognitive mastery. Trainees must calm panicked citizens, extract coordinates, guide pre-arrival actions, and deploy fire engines, ambulances, or police units.

Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT) Model

BKT tracks the latent knowledge state $P(L_t)$ of each learner across 4 competencies:

P(L_{t}|Correct) = \frac{P(L_t)(1 - P(S))}{P(L_t)(1 - P(S)) + (1 - P(L_t))P(G)} P(L_{t+1}) = P(L_{t}|Obs) + (1 - P(L_{t}|Obs)) \cdot P(T)

Where $P(L_0)$ is initial knowledge, $P(T)$ is transition rate, $P(G)$ is guess probability, and $P(S)$ is slip probability.
